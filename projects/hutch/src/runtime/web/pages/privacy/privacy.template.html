Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Who operates Readplace

Readplace is a read-it-later service — the website, the browser extensions, and the iOS and Android apps — operated by Proficient Pty Ltd in Australia; where this policy says what Readplace does, "Readplace" means that operator. The website is readplace.com.

What data Readplace collects

  • Account information: your email address, used for authentication, account recovery, and the emails Readplace sends you about your account and your saved articles, and the name you add on your Account page, if any, which Readplace shows in place of your email.
  • Saved articles: URLs, titles, and metadata of articles you save to your reading list. When you save from a browser extension or the iOS or Android app, the app also sends the page as your device showed it, and a PDF you share to Readplace is uploaded too.
  • Highlights and notes: the passages you highlight in the reader and any note you add to one, stored with your account. They appear in your reader's markdown and EPUB downloads and in your account data export.
  • Files uploaded before signing up: the file, its name and size, and what Readplace reads from it: a book's title, authors and chapter titles, or an audio file's length.
  • Usage data: basic server logs (IP address, request timestamps) necessary to operate and secure the service.
  • Connected app sign-ins: when you connect an app or an AI assistant, Readplace records the time you approved it, the browser and operating system you approved it from, and the city and country your network address pointed to at that moment. These show under Connected apps on your Account page and are deleted when that sign-in ends.
  • App sign-in diagnostics: the iOS and Android apps keep a random id for each installation, sent with each request to tie your sign-in to that device, and report each sign-in step (what happened, its outcome and status code, the app version, and a random diagnostics id) so Readplace can find sign-in failures. They carry no article or reading content.
  • Attribution data: an anonymous visitor id, and the campaign or referrer you first arrived from, joined to a hashed form of your email when you sign up so Readplace can tell which visits led to accounts.

What Readplace does not collect

  • {{icon "x"}}Readplace does not use third-party analytics or tracking scripts.
  • {{icon "x"}}Readplace does not sell or rent your personal data, and shares it only with the providers that run the service: AWS (hosting, and transcription of audio and video files), Stripe (payments), Resend (email), and an AI provider (article text, to clean it up and write the summary).
  • {{icon "x"}}The operator of Readplace does not read the content of the articles you save — article text is sent to an AI provider to pick and clean up the readable text and to write the summary and excerpt on your cards. For newsletters, each issue's subject, sender, link addresses and link text are sent to an AI provider to tell articles from ads and menus, and, for a readlist you described, what you wrote and the articles you kept in it or took out of it are sent with them to choose which fit.

How your data is used

Readplace uses your data solely to provide the Readplace service: authenticating your account, storing your reading list, delivering the web application to your browser, and emailing you about your account and when an article you were waiting on is ready.

Where your data is stored

Your data is stored on AWS infrastructure in the Asia-Pacific (Sydney) region. Readplace uses industry-standard security practices to protect your data.

Connected Gmail accounts

If you connect Gmail, Readplace requests permission to manage forwarding filters and read message metadata. To help you choose senders, it reads the From header, labels and date of about the 5,000 most recent messages in your mailbox outside Spam and Trash and lists the senders of the mail you received (drafts and messages that are only in Sent are left out after those fields are read). While Gmail stays connected, it reads the same fields of new messages, Spam and Trash included, every six hours to keep that list current. When you load senders again, it reads the same fields of messages that arrived or changed labels since the last load, including ones moved to Spam or Trash. At most once every seven days, it emails you a suggestion naming up to 10 newsletters it found, when one of them sends new mail or a sender it has already seen joins our catalog. None of this requests message subjects, bodies or attachments.

Two options ask for more access, and only when you use them. Import unread messages asks for permission to read your email, unless you have already allowed Readplace to modify it. Readplace uses that access only to find the unread messages from the senders you chose, by reading the sender, labels and date of each message Gmail's search returns for a sender's address, and to download and save those messages to your Readplace inbox, where they are handled like forwarded newsletters. Marking imported emails as read asks for permission to modify your email, and Readplace uses it only to find in Gmail the emails it saved from your chosen senders, mark them as read and add the imported-to-readplace label, which it creates if it is missing, and, if you have not given permission to read your email, to fetch the messages that Import unread messages brings in. Readplace never sends or deletes your email.

Readplace stores the discovered sender names and email addresses, your chosen forwarding mappings, and the authorization token needed to keep the connection working, as well as the records of the six-hourly check (such as which senders it saw, where it stopped reading and when it last emailed you). Messages you choose to forward to a Readplace inbox are received and stored separately so you can read them in Readplace. Import unread messages and Marking imported emails as read each keep records of their work: your Gmail address, each sender's address, the Readplace addresses the messages are delivered to, the dates an import covers, when you turned marking on, identifiers of the messages, where each run stopped, and what happened to each message and when. Import unread messages also stores the original copy of each message it downloads and deletes that copy within 31 days, whether or not Gmail stays connected. Disconnecting Gmail asks Google to remove the Readplace filter, revokes Readplace's access to your account and deletes the stored credentials, the discovered sender list, the six-hourly check's records and the import and mark-as-read records. Your chosen forwarding mappings stay stored against that Gmail address, so connecting the same Gmail account again brings them back. Deleting your Readplace account does all of that and also erases those mappings. Messages already imported or forwarded stay in your Readplace inbox after you disconnect, until you delete your account. To disconnect, open the From Gmail page and press Disconnect Gmail. Readplace cannot remove the forwarding address you added in Gmail, so remove it yourself under Forwarding and POP/IMAP.

Readplace's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Readplace does not use Gmail data for advertising, does not sell it, and does not use it to train AI models.

Connected AI assistants

You can connect an AI assistant — Claude, ChatGPT, or another client that speaks the Model Context Protocol — to Readplace. Connecting runs a standard OAuth sign-in in which you approve the connection; the assistant never receives your Readplace password, only a token that acts on your behalf.

A connected assistant can save links to your readlists, list what you have saved, list your readlists, create a readlist and add a saved article to one, read a saved article's details, reader text and summary, see which of your other saves relate to it, and mark an article read or unread. It cannot delete anything, and it cannot reach any other account's data. Whatever you type into the assistant is handled by whoever operates that assistant, under their privacy policy rather than this one.

For each call from a connected assistant, Readplace records which of the actions above the assistant asked for, whether that action succeeded, which assistant asked, and — when it saves a link — the website the link points to. Readplace does not record what you typed to the assistant, the wording of its request, or the full address of a saved link. It uses this only to see which assistants work and where they fail, and keeps it for the same period as the server logs below.

Sign an assistant out under Connected apps on your Account page, or from the assistant's own settings; either revokes its Readplace token. Changing or resetting your password, or deleting your Readplace account, revokes every connected assistant at once.

Data retention and export

Readplace keeps your account and saved articles until you delete them or close your account. Highlights and notes are deleted with the article, and with your account. Server logs are kept for 30 days, app sign-in diagnostics for a year, a login session ends after 180 days without a visit, and export downloads are deleted 7 days after you generate them. Usage events, which carry a visitor id and a hashed email rather than your address, are kept indefinitely. A file you upload before signing up, with its name and what was read from it, is deleted within 2 days. Until you save it no account holds them, so you can't delete them sooner. Once you save it to your library, the file stays with your saved articles until you remove it; an audio or video file's transcript stays with it, and the transcription service's own copy of that transcript is deleted after 14 days. What Readplace keeps from a connected Gmail account, and when it deletes each part, is set out under Connected Gmail accounts. Database backups keep a deleted record for up to 35 days.

You can export a list of your saved articles, with their highlights and notes, at any time from the Export page.

Cookies

Readplace uses first-party cookies only — one to keep you logged in (180 days, renewed as you use the site), an anonymous id to measure how visitors use the site (1 year), a record of the campaign or referrer you arrived from (30 days), which version of the homepage you were shown during a test (1 year), and functional cookies for sign-in state, pending saves, imports, extension detection, and banners you have dismissed. No advertising, third-party, or cross-site tracking cookies.

Your rights

You have the right to access, correct, export, or delete your personal data at any time. You can export your data from the Export page and permanently delete your account — along with your saved list, your highlights and notes, and your reading history — from your Account page. Contact Readplace at the address below for anything else.

Deleting your account

You can delete your Readplace account yourself, from the website or from the iOS or Android app:

  1. Sign in and open your Account page. In the app, open Account.
  2. Under Danger zone, choose Delete account.
  3. Type delete my account permanently and confirm.

Deletion erases your account, your saved list and reading history, your newsletter inbox and forwarding addresses, your Gmail connection and its stored data, your exports, and your billing record, and it signs out every app and connected assistant. A saved article's text is erased too, unless another reader has saved the same page. Server logs are kept for up to 30 days and app sign-in diagnostics for up to a year after deletion. Usage events, which carry a hashed email rather than your address, are kept. A deleted forwarding address stays reserved, with your details removed, so it can never be given to someone else.

If you can no longer sign in, email readplace+privacy@readplace.com from your account's address and Readplace will delete it for you.
