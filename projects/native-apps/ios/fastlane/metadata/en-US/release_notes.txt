Readplace now opens Hand Picked at launch. Every article you save is added to it. To add an older save, use “Add to readlist” on the web. The Hand Picked screen links to it. Open All to see every save, including newsletter links and imports.

Cards now show where an article came from, such as the newsletter that sent it, and how to get back a link you removed.

When your free trial ends, Readplace now moves you to a free plan instead of making your account read-only. You keep every article you saved and can save up to 28 new ones a day. When you reach the day's limit, sharing to Readplace says so plainly, and uploads waiting in the app try again later instead of failing. A subscription adds the Inbox, Next read, Past reads, readlist purpose filtering and Gmail.

Highlights are easier to work with in the reader. A highlight now appears the moment you press Highlight. Tap a highlight to manage it where it sits, see its note beside it, and delete a note with its own button. On a phone, the Highlight bar sits at the bottom of the screen. When an article changes, your highlights follow the edited passage, and any we can't find again are kept and marked "Not found in this version".

You can add an article to several readlists at once and keep up to 15 readlists. You can share the original link from the reader, and report an article that looks wrong. Images keep their space while they load. Download unread waits for articles that are still being prepared, and an offline list tells you when a downloaded article has no text to read. Banners, buttons and the sign-in screen are easier to read. Deleting an article now says it leaves only that readlist. The first time you mark an article read or unread in the reader, Readplace explains that read status is shared across your readlists.

On iOS 26.2 and later, Readplace asks your device for your age range once each launch. You can decline to share it. Readplace is for people 13 and over, so a younger reader sees that instead of the sign-in screen.
