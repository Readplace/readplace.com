Open tabs are the reading list few people admit to keeping. An article looks worth the time, there is none to spare right now, so the tab stays open as a note to come back to.

The window fills. Somewhere past tab 15 the favicons shrink to slivers, and the note stops working. You can no longer tell the saved-for-later from the half-read.

## The browser is the wrong shelf for it

A crash takes the lot. So does a restart for an overnight update. Close the wrong window at the end of the day and a week of things you meant to read goes with it. Those 40 live tabs are also 40 pages the browser keeps open and rendering, spending memory and battery on reading you will mostly do once, if at all.

A tab holds the live page, not a copy of it. So the day you finally come back, the article can sit behind a paywall that was not there when you opened it, or turn up rewritten, or gone to a 404.

A row of open tabs feels like saved reading.

> **It is really a row of links to pages free to change while your back is turned.**

## One keystroke for the whole window

Saving those tabs one at a time means returning to each one and clicking save, 40 times over. The wall of clicks is why the tabs usually win.

Readplace's browser extension now saves every tab in the current window at once. Press Ctrl+Shift+D, or Cmd+Shift+D on a Mac, and the whole window goes to your reading queue. It works the same in Chrome and Firefox.

Each tab gets the treatment a single save gets. Readplace opens the page on its own servers, builds the clean reader copy, stores the images on its own host, and writes the short summary. So 40 tabs do not become 40 bookmarks. They become 40 saved articles, each one readable later off your copy, even after the source moves or goes offline.

The cards land in your queue right away with the site name showing, then fill in their titles and text over the next moments. That is the same pipeline that runs when you save one link by hand, so a tab saved in bulk is no lesser a save than one you do on its own.

## What it skips, and what it tells you

It does not try to save the whole window blind. A tab parked on a browser settings page, a local file, an extension page, or Readplace itself is not an article, so the save steps over it. Two tabs open on the same address save once, not twice.

When it finishes, the popup hands you a tally rather than a shrug: Saved, Skipped, and Failed, with a button straight to your queue. A page whose captured text runs over the size cap is kept as a link instead of dropped, and the tally names it with its size, so you know which few came in as links rather than full copies.

None of that asks for a decision mid-save. You press the keys, the window empties into the queue, and the summary says where each tab went.

## Clear the window into your queue

Keeping 40 tabs open says nothing about discipline. It is what reading worth doing looks like when the day has no room for it and the browser is the only shelf within reach. The tabs are a reading list. They are kept in the one place that keeps losing them.

So move the list somewhere built to hold it. Saved off the live page, your reading stops riding on 40 fragile sessions and a browser that has not crashed yet.
