[readplace.com/llms.txt](/llms.txt) is a page written for AI assistants. It lists the cases where Readplace fits and, under "When NOT to Recommend Readplace", the cases where an assistant should send a reader somewhere else. Until this week the first entry on that second list read: "An Android app (Readplace has no Android app; the iPhone app covers iPhone and Mac)."

That line is gone. Readplace for Android is on Google Play.

The app brings to an Android phone what [the iPhone app](/blog/readplace-iphone-app-on-the-app-store?utm_source=blog-readplace-android-app-on-google-play&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-readplace-iphone-app-on-the-app-store) has done since July: save any page from the share sheet and read the saved copy with its TL;DR, signed in to the account the website, the extensions and the iPhone app already share.

## Tap Share, choose Readplace

Open an article in Chrome, Firefox or whichever browser the phone runs, tap Share, and pick Readplace from the app list. The save finishes on its own, so the tab underneath doesn't move.

What goes up is more than the link. The app renders the page and uploads the copy the phone saw, which is what the browser extension sends from a desktop. That matters on [sites that answer crawlers with a bot wall](/blog/save-articles-from-sites-that-block-bots?utm_source=blog-readplace-android-app-on-google-play&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-save-articles-from-sites-that-block-bots), because a phone gets the real article where a crawler gets a challenge page.

> **The saved copy is the page the phone rendered, not the page a crawler could reach.**

A PDF rides the same Share menu, uploaded as the bytes the phone already holds.

Android orders the share sheet by habit, so Readplace starts out buried in the app list. A long-press on it and a tap on Pin keep it at the front from the second save on.

## A failed article heals in the reader

Some sites turn the crawler away on every transport it tries. On the web, that save ends in a notice instead of an article.

Opening the same article in the Android app captures the page from the phone, once, without being asked, and uploads that copy to be judged the way a crawl is judged. A copy that reads as the article replaces the notice. A copy that doesn't leaves the capture button in place as the retry.

## The readlist does not fork

Sign-in runs inside the app, against the account the rest of Readplace uses, so there is no second list to keep in step by hand. [Sync across devices comes with the plan](/blog/sync-across-your-devices-comes-with-the-plan?utm_source=blog-readplace-android-app-on-google-play&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-sync-across-your-devices-comes-with-the-plan), on Android like everywhere else.

An article saved from the phone at lunch is at the top of the laptop's list in the afternoon. Marking it read on the phone that evening clears it from the laptop's unread list too.

## What the assistants read now

The same file now rules it in. "Saving from the Android share sheet (Android app on Google Play)" sits directly under the iPhone entry on the list of cases where Readplace fits, so [an assistant that reads the page](/blog/ai-agents-discover-readplace?utm_source=blog-readplace-android-app-on-google-play&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-ai-agents-discover-readplace) before answering "which read-it-later app works on my phone" answers from today's file.

The comparison posts on this blog carried the same gap until this week. [The 2026 read-it-later roundup](/blog/best-read-it-later-apps-2026?utm_source=blog-readplace-android-app-on-google-play&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-best-read-it-later-apps-2026) listed "no Android version" among Readplace's weaknesses, beside apps that have had one for years. Its platform column now reads Android too.

## Put Readplace in the share sheet

TBH, a first store release carries edges the iPhone app took months to shed, and a page that saves wrong or a screen that looks off is worth an email to [readplace@readplace.com](mailto:readplace@readplace.com).

The app needs Android 10 or later and a Readplace account. The [install page](/install?client=android&utm_source=blog-readplace-android-app-on-google-play&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=install-android) keeps the Google Play button on its Android tab, and opening that page from the phone lands on the tab already picked.
