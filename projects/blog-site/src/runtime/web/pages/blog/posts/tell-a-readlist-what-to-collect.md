The readlist create dialog grew a switch this week: Automatically add matching articles. Under it sits one question, "What should this readlist collect?", and the answer becomes a standing instruction. A list created with the switch on is a Smart Readlist, and the filing is its job from then on.

Filing by hand is the half of the habit that kept slipping. A link gets saved the second it's found, and the sorting into [readlists](/blog/sort-your-saves-into-readlists?utm_source=blog-tell-a-readlist-what-to-collect&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-sort-your-saves-into-readlists) waits for a quieter moment that rarely comes. A few weeks of that, and a list named for a subject holds whatever the last tidy-up reached.

> **The tap saves the link. The instructions decide where else it lands.**

## One instruction, every source

Every save lands in All first, as before. What's new is that a Smart Readlist watches the arrivals: the page sent from the Chrome or Firefox extension, the share from the iPhone or Android app, a link [a script saved over the API](/blog/build-on-the-readplace-api?utm_source=blog-tell-a-readlist-what-to-collect&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-build-on-the-readplace-api), an import, the links inside a newsletter. Readplace checks each new save against the instructions of every Smart Readlist on the account, and each list takes the ones that fit.

Email got there first. A readlist's purpose has been sorting [newsletter articles into the list they belong to](/blog/newsletter-articles-land-in-the-readlist-they-belong-to?utm_source=blog-tell-a-readlist-what-to-collect&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-newsletter-articles-land-in-the-readlist-they-belong-to) since early November. The switch widens that to every way in, so the instruction now meets each save, not only the ones that came by mail.

## What the decision reads

The check waits for the crawl to settle, because it reads the words a reader would see: the article's title and its opening excerpt, with the URL beside them. A save that came by email brings its subject line and sender along.

Where the reader has sorted that list's email suggestions before, the 20 newest Keep and Doesn't fit calls ride along as examples of what the list takes and what it turns away.

3 verdicts come back: keep, drop or unsure.

Keep files the article, already marked read if it was read between the save and the decision. Drop leaves the save in All. Unsure does too, on purpose: in a list meant to stay on topic, a doubtful add costs more than a missing one, and the missing one is still in All.

## Adding is all it does

The matcher only adds.

An article leaves a Smart Readlist when the reader takes it out, and the removal sticks: the same link is not filed into that list again.

The past stays where it was put, too. Instructions apply to the saves that come after them, so editing the rule changes the next save and leaves the backlog as the reader shelved it.

Switching smart matching off takes nothing with it. The articles already filed stay where they are, with their read status, and new ones arrive only by hand through [the Add dialog](/blog/add-an-article-to-several-readlists-at-once?utm_source=blog-tell-a-readlist-what-to-collect&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-add-an-article-to-several-readlists-at-once).

## Matching pauses on the free plan

Each decision is a model call, so Smart Readlists belong to the paid plan. On [the free plan](/blog/readplace-free-plan?utm_source=blog-tell-a-readlist-what-to-collect&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-readplace-free-plan) the list says so plainly: smart matching is paused, upgrade to match new saves again, or turn it off. What the list already holds keeps opening while it waits.

## Make one list smart

The strongest first candidate is the list whose name is nearly an instruction by itself: a city, a technology, the research behind a long project. Open a custom readlist's Preferences and choose Make this a Smart Readlist, or tick the switch while creating a new one.

The next save is the test. Make the list at [readplace.com](/?utm_source=blog-tell-a-readlist-what-to-collect&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=home), write its one instruction, and save something that fits.
