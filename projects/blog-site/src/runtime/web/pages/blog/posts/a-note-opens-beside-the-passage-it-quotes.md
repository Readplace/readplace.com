Add note asked for words about a passage it had just hidden. The editor opened as a dialog in the centre of the screen, the article behind it went dim and blurred, and the highlighted sentence sat somewhere behind the blur. Writing about it meant remembering it.

The editor moved. It's now a small panel that opens clear of the passage, above it when there's room and below it when there isn't, and nothing dims around it.

> **A note about a sentence belongs where the sentence can still be read.**

## The panel that replaced the dialog

The passage comes along. Its words appear quoted at the top of the panel, with the note field and its buttons under them, so the sentence under discussion and the sentence being written about it share one small frame.

Scrolling doesn't shake it off. The panel follows its passage down the page, and when neither side has room it docks at the bottom edge of the visible page, quote and all. On a phone that edge is the visible one, so the docked panel sits above the keyboard rather than under it.

## Reading goes on around it

A dialog that dims the page also claims every click, because anywhere outside it means closing it. The panel makes the opposite deal. A click in the article leaves it open, so checking a paragraph 3 screens up doesn't cost a half-written note. The draft waits in the panel, and the panel stays with its passage.

That matters most on the notes that take a while... the ones where the sentence gets reread 4 times before the first word of the answer lands.

## A shared link carries the same pen

A shared Readplace page already lets a visitor without an account [keep up to 5 highlights in the browser](/blog/highlight-a-shared-article-without-an-account?utm_source=blog-a-note-opens-beside-the-passage-it-quotes&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-highlight-a-shared-article-without-an-account). Managing them was another matter. A kept mark answered nothing on hover or click, couldn't be reached from the keyboard or described by a screen reader, and its note went away only by opening the form and deleting the text.

The shared page now renders the reader's own controls. Hovering or tapping a mark opens the preview and its actions, Add note opens the same panel beside the passage, and Delete highlight asks before anything goes. The rows of the Highlights card gained a Delete note of their own. All of it stays in the browser, like the marks themselves, until an account claims them.

One background job lost its claws along the way. The shared page polls for the article's TL;DR while one is still being written, and each answer used to rebuild every mark and row, wiping a note mid-type. An answer that brings nothing new now touches nothing.

## Beside the next highlight

A note written tonight outlives tonight's copy of the article: when a fresh crawl replaces the body, [the highlight under it finds its sentence again](/blog/highlights-and-notes-stay-on-their-passage?utm_source=blog-a-note-opens-beside-the-passage-it-quotes&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-highlights-and-notes-stay-on-their-passage) and the note rides with it.

Select a sentence in any article on [the readlist](/queue?utm_source=blog-a-note-opens-beside-the-passage-it-quotes&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=readlist) and the toolbar offers Add note. On a shared link the same selection works signed out, with the first 5 marks held by the browser. A saved article is the only thing the panel needs, and [readplace.com](/?utm_source=blog-a-note-opens-beside-the-passage-it-quotes&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=home) turns any link into one.
