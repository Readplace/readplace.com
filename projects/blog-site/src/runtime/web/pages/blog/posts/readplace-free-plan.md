Read-only was the state an unpaid Readplace account ended up in. The 14 days with the paid plan ran out, the saved articles stayed open, and the Save button stopped saving.

That state is gone. An account that has not paid, this week or ever, saves again within a daily limit.

## An account that never paid saves again

Take an account made in September that let its 14 days end without adding a card. Until this week it could open the articles it already held and add nothing new.

Today the same account sends a link from the browser extension, and the article lands in its readlist with a reader view and a TL;DR, like any other save. The account is the same one it was in September. What changed is the plan an unpaid account lands on.

> **Reading what you saved has no limit. Adding to it has a daily one.**

## Opening a saved article costs nothing

Reading is free on the free plan, and so are highlights, notes and the TL;DR at the top of each article. An article already saved opens as often as it's wanted, because opening it does none of the work a save does.

New saves are where the work is. Readplace fetches each new link, cleans it into a reader view and writes its summary, so the free plan takes a limited number of new saves a day. The limit counts every way in: the browser extension, the iPhone and Android apps, an import, an AI assistant, and a new link opened while signed in.

Pages read by OCR, book uploads, AI assistant tool calls and exports have daily limits of their own. When an account reaches one, Readplace says so, and the saved articles keep opening.

An import larger than a day's allowance keeps its review. The links over the limit stay selected, ready to save the next day.

## 5 features stay on the paid plan

The paid plan lifts those daily limits and adds the Inbox with its newsletter addresses, Next read, Past reads, readlist purpose filtering and Gmail. Every new account has it for its first 14 days, with no card asked for. After that, the account stays on the free plan unless it upgrades.
