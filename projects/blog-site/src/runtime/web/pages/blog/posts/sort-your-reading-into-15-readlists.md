Finance, AI, Cities, Recipes, Longreads, Work, and one called Someday. That's 7 readlists, and 7 was the cap: the limit dialog offered no way forward except deleting a list to make room for the next.

The cap is 15 now.

The number was the small part of the change. A sidebar that lists 15 readlists in a column has outgrown the word sidebar, and a dialog that asks for a destination gets slower to scan as the choices double. Most of what shipped keeps the 15 reachable, not just allowed.

> **Raising the cap took 1 line, and the rest of the change keeps every readlist at most 2 taps away.**

Nothing changes for an account holding 7 readlists or fewer: the sidebar keeps listing every one of them, as before. The 8th readlist is where the new layout starts.

## Pin the 4 worth a fixed spot

Past 7 readlists, the sidebar stops listing the lot. It shows All, then up to 4 rows the reader chooses, and choosing is called pinning: each readlist's menu, next to Edit and Delete, now carries Pin.

A pinned readlist keeps its row on each page until it's unpinned. A 5th pin is refused: "You can pin up to 4 readlists. Unpin one before pinning another." The short column is the point.

Pinning is optional. Slots nobody filled take the first readlists in order, so a reader who leaves the menu alone still gets a full set of rows. And the readlist that's open keeps a row of its own, pinned or not, so the sidebar can't lose the page it's standing on.

## The rest wait one row down

Under the pinned rows sits More readlists, with a count beside it. On desktop it opens a panel next to the sidebar. On a phone the same panel arrives as a sheet.

Every readlist is in there. When the panel grows tall enough to scroll, a search field appears at its top, typing narrows the rows letter by letter, and Enter opens the first match. A readlist that didn't earn a pin is a few keystrokes away instead of a scroll away.

Create a readlist sits in the panel too, so the view that shows each list is also where a new one starts.

## Pickers stopped trading buttons for rows

The sidebar isn't the only surface that lists readlists. A card's Move to and Add to dialogs ask for a destination, the reader files an article from its own page, the mark-as-read confirmation names each readlist the article sits in, and the Gmail integration maps a sender to the readlist its issues feed.

Each of those used to grow taller with the account. Now the rows scroll while the dialog's buttons hold still, and past 10 readlists a filter appears above the rows, the same one the More panel uses. Typing "ci" finds Cities without reading the list at all.

## Newsletters are why 7 ran out

A readlist in Readplace is closer to plumbing than to a folder. A custom email address can feed one, a [Gmail sender](/blog/saved-newsletters-stop-sitting-unread-in-gmail?utm_source=blog-sort-your-reading-into-15-readlists&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-saved-newsletters-stop-sitting-unread-in-gmail) can save each issue's article links into one, and [a written purpose](/blog/newsletter-articles-land-in-the-readlist-they-belong-to?utm_source=blog-sort-your-reading-into-15-readlists&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-newsletter-articles-land-in-the-readlist-they-belong-to) lets AI file the links that fit it. Each of those is set up on 1 readlist's own Preferences tab.

So a topic that earns its own feed earns its own list, and a reader following 10 topics had to merge 3 of them.

The comparison tables kept the score. Against [Omnivore](/blog/omnivore-alternative?utm_source=blog-sort-your-reading-into-15-readlists&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-omnivore-alternative) and in [the Pocket migration guide](/blog/pocket-migration?utm_source=blog-sort-your-reading-into-15-readlists&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=post-pocket-migration), the row where other apps answer "tags" used to read "Readlists, up to 7". It reads up to 15 now. Readlists are the one structure a saved article gets here, so the cap is the size of the whole filing system.

15 is still a cap. An account that wants a 16th meets the same dialog with a bigger number in it.

## Make the 8th readlist

The 8th readlist is the one the limit dialog used to refuse, and it's the one that flips the sidebar into pinned rows, so creating it shows the whole change at once.

Create a readlist waits at the bottom of the sidebar on [the readlist page](/queue?utm_source=blog-sort-your-reading-into-15-readlists&utm_medium=internal&utm_content=readlist), and it keeps saying yes until the 15th. Pin the 4 that matter, and let the other 11 wait one row down.
