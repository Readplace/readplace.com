Tab hoarders run a filing system they never agreed to. The open tab is the reminder. The window crowded with them is the to-read pile.

It works, mostly. A tab stays open as a standing note: read this when there's time. Twenty of them and the window is a backlog you can scroll. You stop closing tabs because each one is a thing you meant to get to, and closing it feels like deciding you never will.

I have had the same 40 tabs open across two laptops.

## One reboot empties the pile

Then the machine reboots for an update. Or the browser runs out of memory and drops the lot. Or you press restore and a third of them come back blank, the sites already past the version you saved.

The pile was never written down anywhere. It lived in one program's memory, and memory is the first thing a restart clears.

> **The open tab is the only copy, and a reboot is all it takes to lose it.**

## Send the whole window to your queue

Right-click any page and pick Save All Tabs to Readplace. The keyboard shortcut is Ctrl+Shift+D, Command+Shift+D on a Mac. Either one reads every tab in the current window and saves them together.

Before anything goes out, the extension trims the list. A new-tab page, a settings screen, a local file, the Readplace tab itself, none of those are articles, so they get dropped on your side and never reach the server. Two tabs open on the same address collapse to one. What's left is the reading.

Then it saves. A window of 50 tabs goes up in batches of 20, so one heavy window does not become one request the server turns down. Each page saves the way a single save works: the card shows the site name at once, and the title, the reader text, and the summary fill in over the next moments.

When it finishes you get a line that counts the result. Saved 18 · Skipped 4. A page too large to copy in full, the odd one past 20 MB, is kept as a link with a note that says so, rather than sinking the whole batch.

## A bookmark is just the address

Saving open tabs is an old trick. Every browser has Bookmark All Tabs, and on the surface it has the same shape: one action, the whole window filed.

The difference is what gets kept. A bookmark stores the address and nothing else. Open it in six months and you are back on the live web, at whatever the page has become, a paywall, a redirect, a 404 where an article used to be.

Save the same tabs to Readplace and each one becomes a copy on Readplace's servers, the cleaned text and the images, held apart from the source. [Saved articles outlast the original page](/blog/saved-articles-outlast-the-original-page), so the tab you close today reads the same next month whether or not the site is still standing.

> **Bookmarking all your tabs keeps the addresses, saving them to Readplace keeps the pages.**

## The window can finally close

The reason the tabs stay open is trust. You keep them in front of you because the open tab is the one place you are sure the page still is. Close it and, in your experience, the thing tends to evaporate.

Saving the window takes that bet off the table. The pages are held somewhere that survives a reboot, a crash, an update on a Tuesday morning you did not ask for. The queue waits at [readplace.com](/) and reads back the same on your phone.

So the tabs can go, not because you gave up on reading them, but because closing one no longer means losing it.
